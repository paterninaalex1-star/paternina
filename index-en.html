Case Study.

How we turned a candy into a story people want to hear.

Project

De mi Tierra / CuCriosity Labs

Role

Branding · UX/UI · Strategy

Year

2024 – 2025

During Holy Week, in Parque Suri Salcedo, there are women who have been selling sweets for decades. Their recipes are heritage. Their story, invisible. That was the problem.

The insight that changed everything.

The consumer doesn't buy the candy. They buy the memory of what that candy represents. When we understood that, everything changed: we weren't designing an online store, we were building a cultural archive with a sales interface.

The solution.

A digital platform where the matrons don't just sell — they tell stories. Brand identity, content system and platform proposal built from field research, not from a desk. The result was a brand with history, a product with context, and a community with visibility.

Case Study · Market Research

Zentra Study.

How we understood a market nobody had truly listened to.

Project

Market Research — Virtual Psychotherapy

Methodology

Mixed · 4 interviews + 100 surveys

Scope

Barranquilla · Cartagena · Santa Marta

The virtual psychotherapy market on the Colombian Caribbean Coast isn't saturated. It's poorly solved. That was the first thing we found when we started researching: users don't reject virtual sessions or prices within reasonable ranges. They reject paying for a one-sided conversation with a professional who doesn't listen.

The methodology.

We combined 4 in-depth qualitative interviews with 100 quantitative responses in Barranquilla, Cartagena and Santa Marta. The goal wasn't to confirm hypotheses — it was to understand the exact language users use to describe their pain, their objections and their ideal experience. That difference changes everything.

The findings that redefined the strategy.

The market doesn't look for "therapy". It looks to feel understood. 43% of respondents would only seek professional help when their personal strategies fail. The insight isn't "sell therapy" — it's to position as the space where someone finally listens.

Active listening is the most valued differentiator and the least delivered. 60% value a psychologist who listens actively, but 3 out of 4 interviewees reported experiences where the professional "talked and talked and talked" without leaving any space. No one in the market is filling that competitive gap.

Trust is built on social media before the first payment. 41% discover mental health services through Instagram and 31% through TikTok. The conclusion was clear: "I need to see video, at least... I won't trust someone who doesn't post videos." The funnel starts months before the first session.

The fair price is between $80,000 and $120,000 COP. 62% are willing to pay in that range. Beyond $200,000 generates strong resistance. Recommended pricing strategy: exploratory session at $50,000 to break the first-payment barrier, then 4-session packages with a 10–15% discount to anchor monthly commitment.

The Caribbean "tough it out" culture is barrier #1. The quantitative scale shows high agreement (3.82/5) that there's more stigma around mental health on the Caribbean Coast. The interviews capture the exact language: "Here they joke a lot about it," "man up," "just get over it." Zentra's communication couldn't directly confront that stigma — it had to reframe it.

The resulting strategy.

Based on the 10 main insights, we built a five-pillar content strategy: emotional identification, practical education, authority with a face, cultural normalization and soft conversion. The tone wouldn't be clinical ("treatment", "patient") but close ("emotional care", "talking to someone who listens"). All texts, hooks and calls to action for Zentra came directly from the literal phrases we heard in the interviews — not from desk assumptions.
